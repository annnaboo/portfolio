How It Thinks

The plugin is built around three ideas: one page holds the truth, every update belongs to a release, and nothing is replaced without a review.

Decisions & Consequences

Small tool, real tradeoffs.

Decision 01
Support frames, not only components
Migrating the team to components would be the “clean” answer, but it isn’t how the team works today.
Adoption · Reality
What happened
The plugin fits the existing workflow — nobody has to change how they file updates.
Decision 02
Exact copy, never a redraw
Replacement duplicates the shipped frame instead of rebuilding it.
Fidelity
What happened
What ships is pixel-identical everywhere it appears.
Decision 03
Private first, Community later
I’m using it on real files before publishing.
Validation
What happened
Edge cases surface on real work, not in a demo file.
Decision 04
Two languages from day one
A click-to-switch RU / EN interface.
Accessibility · Team
What happened
The whole team can use it in the language they think in.

06

What I learned

Design ops problems are rarely about pixels. They are about keeping many copies of the truth in sync.

Tools should meet teams where they are. A plugin that requires a new workflow is one more thing to adopt; a plugin that fits the old one just gets used.

The smallest tools often save the most time — because they remove a task people do every single release.

Building it myself with an AI partner moved me from “I wish Figma did this” to a working tool in my own file.
