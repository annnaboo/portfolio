Decisions & Consequences

Each decision came with a tradeoff. Here is what I chose and what it led to.

Decision 01
Show the real app, not a quiz
An early version branched into a quiz. It drifted from the main idea, so I rebuilt the flow on the interactive demo: record → transcribe → summary → share.
Honesty · Activation
What happened
The onboarding now previews exactly what users get after the paywall — no promises the product can’t keep.
Decision 02
One scenario first, then swap
Instead of designing four onboardings in parallel, I finished the Team-meeting flow end to end and agreed it with the team.
Process · Efficiency
What happened
All 8 Team-meeting animations were approved and written up as a motion spec. Other scenarios reuse it and only swap texts and colours.
Decision 03
Animate the stage, freeze the UI
Text and buttons stay static; only the scene moves. Screens advance on tap, so every animation loops while it waits.
Readability · 45+
What happened
Users read at their own pace — nothing moves under the text they are reading.
Decision 04
Copy that matches the product
No sync or cloud promises in account copy, no reminders in the flow, and “AI Summary” as the only product name.
Trust · Content
What happened
Sign-in, onboarding and paywall tell one consistent story that the product actually delivers.

06

What I learned

The hardest part of an AI product is not the AI. It is making the value visible in the first minute — before the user has recorded anything of their own.

An onboarding that plays the real flow does that better than any feature list. Show the summary, not the promise of a summary.

Motion is not decoration here. It is how a 45+ user understands what just happened: voice became text, text became a summary, the summary went to the right people.

Building one scenario to the end before multiplying it saved weeks of rework. It also gave the team one clear thing to approve instead of four half-finished ones.
